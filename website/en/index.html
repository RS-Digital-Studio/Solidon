Three steps to a printed part

Built for everyone who prints — not just for people who know CAD. The way from download to part is short:

  1. Get the demo, start it. No account, no key, no setup — install and go. Ten example projects are waiting on the start screen.
  2. Drag a model in. An STL from Printables or your own project: the report says right away what would go wrong when printing — and every finding carries a suggested action.
  3. Adjust and print. Click a face, change the number, take the fit from the material profile — then export, or hand over to your slicer directly.

Sound familiar?

Solidon3D is not a slicer and not a model catalogue. It sits in between: between the downloaded STL that does not fit and the slicer that prints it without asking. The four sentences heard most often around a printer — and what Solidon3D does about them:

  • The hole is 0.2 mm too small, and I don't have CAD.

    Click the bore, enter the new dimension. Even in a downloaded STL — round bores are recognised as named features and wall thickness is measured. Solidon3D does not invent pockets or lost CAD history.

  • The print failed at 80 %, because of an island.

    Layer analysis shows it beforehand. Overhangs, islands, walls under two extrusion widths and the support volume — before the slicer spends the time.

  • The lid jams. It fitted on the third try.

    Fits from the material profile. Clearance, press fit or flush are derived from a calibration ladder you print once, not guessed.

  • The part is 40 mm too big for the bed.

    Split and pin in one step. Auto split creates the alignment pins, the holes and the matching fits along with them.

Four ways to a part

All four end in the same scene, the same history and the same inspection report. You decide per part, not per program.

Way 1 — Edit

Change a downloaded model

Drop the file — or drag the link from MakerWorld, Printables or Thingiverse straight out of the browser into the window, skipping the download folder. Read the inspection report, click a face or a hole — then say in the chat what it should become, or pick from the context menu. Compare before/after, apply, export.

Way 2 — Design

Build new, with dimensions

Describe what you need. The agent creates parameters and places proven building blocks — threads, heat-set inserts, hinges. Turn the numbers, the model follows instantly.

Way 3 — Generate

From text or image

A generated mesh runs through the repair chain automatically, is brought to a resolution you can work with, gets an inspection report and is split and pinned when needed — until it sits printable on the plate. Needs a locally running ComfyUI and a capable graphics card; the generator model is TripoSG under the MIT licence, and one command sets it up. Without both, ways 1 and 2 remain fully usable.

Way 4 — Sculpting

Figures by hand

Some shapes cannot be dimensioned. Rough out the base from primitives, blend them together, even out the mesh — then sculpt it with six brushes. The whole session stays one step in the history, and editable.

What no sculpting program can do: while you sculpt, wall thickness runs along. Spots that are too thin appear as a number in the bar — not in the slicer once the shape is finished.

What ends up on the bed

Four parts, built and checked in Solidon3D. Each carries a feature no box editor gives you: a thread that grips, a hinge in one piece, a hook that actually hooks. Every one of them was built through the same menus you use — step by step, and every step can be taken back.

All features at a glance

What makes the difference

The essentials in one sentence each — all features with pictures on the features page.

  • An AI agent that does not guess. One sentence in the chat becomes a proposal that a single undo takes back completely. Geometry is computed by code, never by the language model — and that is measured: a suite of 39 reference requests runs before and after every change to the agent's rule set.
  • The face you click is the one that counts. A hole, block or sketch lands where you pointed — every operation on a face used to cost two clicks. A face's context menu now also offers wall mount, rib, tongue and groove, snap tab, snap fit and living hinge.
  • An inspection report before printing. Open edges, flipped normals, walls under two extrusion widths — every finding jumps to the spot in the model and carries a suggested action.
  • Change a number instead of rebuilding. The history is non-destructive: wall thickness from 2.40 to 3.60, and hollowing, lid, holes and fits are recomputed.
  • Tested building blocks, from threads to pegboard hooks. The dimensions come from a standard-parts table, not from a gut feeling — and every block is computed across its whole range.
  • Your own blocks without programming. Build it once, save the selection as a part in the catalogue — with adjustable dimensions and a checked range. It travels as a recipe inside every project file that uses it: steps and values, not a program.
  • Layer analysis without a slicer. Overhangs, islands, bridge spans and support volume; the orientation search suggests the pose that produces the least of it.
  • Constrained sketches. Ten constraints hold the drawing together, and the outline stays an exact curve — a circle becomes a cylinder, not a polygon.

When the model comes from an AI

A generator checks whether the mesh is closed. What it does not check is whether the wall is thicker than your nozzle, whether an overhang holds, whether an island starts in mid-air and whether the whole thing fits your build plate. That is where Solidon3D starts — and afterwards every number can still be changed.

Four of them from a single sentence each, generated and checked in Solidon3D — all closed and in one piece, around twenty seconds apiece. The generator runs on your own machine: no account, no upload, and the model behind it is MIT-licensed.

What Solidon3D is not

Better said up front:
  • Not a slicer. Solidon3D analyses and prepares; the print file still comes from your slicer — Solidon3D hands over directly and reads its G-code back as a cross-check.
  • Not cloud CAD. No accounts, no storage on someone else's server, no telemetry. Projects are files on your machine.
  • No dimensional guarantees from generated meshes. What comes from text or images is made printable — accurate design happens on ways 1 and 2.
  • The AI brings its own cost. The chat runs on your own API key — the provider bills the model use, typically cents per proposal. Running it locally through Ollama is free, but a capable model (around 14 billion parameters and up) needs a graphics card with 16 GB of memory.

Common questions

What happens on 30 October?

From 31 October the demo will no longer start. It says so beforehand: the status bar shows every day how much time is left.

Version 1.0 arrives on 1 November 2026, the paid release. The 31st of October falls in between — for one day there is neither.

Your files are not affected. They stay where you saved them; nothing is deleted and nothing becomes unreadable. A project file is a ZIP archive with JSON inside and opens without Solidon3D — the next version reads it unchanged.

Why a demo with an end date instead of a trial?

Because that lets it be complete. A version that turns into a viewer after fourteen days shows less on your second project than on your first. This one shows everything until the last day — and then ends, rather than living on as half a program that nobody maintains.

Can I create my own building blocks without programming?

Yes. Build the part once, create parameters for its dimensions and choose Save selection as a part in the catalogue. Creating it computes the block across its whole range; afterwards it sits next to the supplied ones with a preview image and fields.

It stays on your computer — and travels as a recipe inside every project file that uses it: a list of steps and values, not a program. Whoever receives the file can compute with it without installing anything.

Do I need CAD experience?

No. Way 1 gets by with clicking and one sentence in the chat, way 2 with describing and turning numbers. And the drawing itself is built for beginners: every tool says what the next click does, and every constraint explains in one sentence what it holds.

Do I have to use AI?

No. The chat is one way out of four. Without a key and without Ollama, everything except the chat stays fully usable — menus, context menus, command line and all 95 operations.

Does the AI compute my geometry?

No, and that is the most important sentence on this page. The language model picks operations and parameters — every piece of geometry is computed by code. Each proposal from the agent is exactly one transaction, and a single undo takes it back completely.

Can I drive Solidon3D from Claude Code or another AI tool?

Yes. Solidon3D has a built-in MCP server: a program like Claude Code uses it to call the same operations that sit in the menus. It is off by default, accepts connections only from your own machine (127.0.0.1), takes neither file paths nor source code from outside — and every remote call is a transaction in the history that a single undo takes back completely.

Do my models go anywhere?

Only if you use the chat with a third-party API key — then a digest of the scene goes to that provider: objects with their dimensions, the names of the features, the parameters and the history in brief. No geometry, no files. With Ollama even that stays local. Without the chat, almost nothing leaves the machine: no accounts, no storage on someone else's server, no telemetry.

Two exceptions remain. At startup, Solidon3D optionally checks whether a newer version is out — the check states only its own version number and can be switched off in the settings. And what you send yourself: a feedback message to support, whose contents you see in a preview beforehand.

Which formats does Solidon3D read and write?

It reads STL, 3MF (including assemblies), OBJ and GLB, plus STEP with real faces and edges. It also reads PLY, OFF and glTF — and, as drawings, SVG and DXF, whose outline becomes a solid. It writes STL, 3MF — with colour changes and plate layout —, OBJ, PLY, GLB and STEP; STEP appears in the export dialog as soon as a solid can carry it. GLB is the format for showing: colours and name travel along, and the recipient turns the part in a browser. A project is a ZIP file with project.json inside: openable without Solidon3D.

Does it run on a Mac?

Yes, as separate builds for Apple Silicon and Intel — a package built on arm64 will not start on an Intel Mac, and that covers every machine made before 2020. It is not yet notarised with Apple, and macOS will not open an installer package without that check on a double-click: it reports that it cannot verify the file is free of malware and offers “Move to Trash” as the first button. Choose “Done” there, then release the package in System Settings under “Privacy & Security” with “Open Anyway”. Notarisation follows once the Apple account is in place.

How do I install the Flatpak on Linux?

A Flatpak is not a double-click installer but a package your package manager takes in. Open a terminal in the folder you downloaded to and enter: flatpak install ./Solidon3D-*.flatpak

Solidon3D then appears in your application menu. You can also start it with flatpak run de.rsdigital.solidon3d and remove it with flatpak uninstall de.rsdigital.solidon3d. If your system does not know the flatpak command, your distribution installs it under exactly that name.

Will it work with my printer?

Sixteen profiles are included, among them Elegoo, Bambu Lab, Prusa, Creality, Anycubic and Sovol. You can add your own — what is needed is the build volume, the nozzle and the layer height. For the fits, one calibration print comes on top.

What happens after version 1.x?

Every 1.x update is included in the purchase. A later major version would be a new purchase — the version you bought keeps running, it does not stop working, and your projects stay readable.
