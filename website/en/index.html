Three steps to a printed part

Built for everyone who prints, not just for those who know CAD. This is what the path looks like in the demo:

  1. Download and start the demo

    No account, no key, no setup. Four guided tours and twelve example projects are waiting on the start screen.

  2. Drag in your model

    An STL from Printables or a project of your own. The report tells you right away what would go wrong when printing, and every finding comes with a suggestion of what to do.

  3. Adjust and hand over

    Click a hole or face, change the measured number, take the fit from the material profile. Then export, or hand it straight to your slicer with settings that suit the part.

Sound familiar?

Four things people often say about printing, and what Solidon3D makes of them:

  • The hole is 0.2 mm too small, and I don't have CAD.

    Click the hole, enter the new size. Even in a downloaded STL: holes are recognised as features, and the wall thickness next to them is measured. Solidon3D does not invent lost CAD history.

  • The print failed at 80 %, because of an island.

    Layer analysis shows it beforehand. Overhangs, islands, walls under two extrusion widths and the support volume — before the slicer spends the time.

  • The lid jams. It fitted on the third try.

    Fits from the material profile. Clearance, press fit or flush are derived from a calibration ladder you print once, not guessed.

  • The part is 40 mm too big for the bed.

    Split and pin in one step. Auto split creates the alignment pins, the holes and the matching fits along with them.

Four ways to a part

All four end in the same scene, the same history and the same inspection report. You decide per part, not per program.

What makes the difference

The essentials at a glance. Every tool, with pictures and examples, is on the features page.

An AI agent that does not guess

One sentence in the chat becomes a proposal that a single undo takes back completely. Geometry is computed by code, never by the language model, and that is measured: a suite of 39 reference requests runs before and after every change to the agent’s rule set.

The hole grows. The entrance follows.

Click a recognised hole to move, turn, duplicate or resize it. If it has a countersink at the top or a step below, you decide what goes along. Every fit that points at it keeps its reference.

An organiser that fits its contents

Divide compartments in the preview, specify outer dimensions or clear compartment sizes and change divider heights individually. Project parameters keep related dimensions equal. Tray, rim, divider and plug-in foot are also available as building blocks.

Hole fields with a border

Distribute round holes, slots or honeycomb cells within a drawn area. Border width, spacing and excluded zones define the field; its dimensions remain editable later.

What is left on the spool

The filament inventory keeps every spool with material, colour, storage location and remaining amount — across projects. After the slicer handover, Solidon3D offers to book the consumption, estimated or planned from the G-code; the history shows every booking and takes it back on request.

Remote control from Claude Code

A built-in MCP server lets a second program call the same operations as the menus. It is off by default and reachable only on your own machine. Every remote call that changes something is a step in the history that one undo takes back.

Also included: constrained sketches, where a circle on an exact body stays a cylinder and never becomes a polygon, reliefs from images, spatial lattices, draft angles, matching lids and multicolour labels.

What Solidon3D is not

Better said up front.

  • Not a slicer. Solidon3D analyses and prepares; the print file still comes from your slicer. Solidon3D hands over directly and reads its G-code back as a cross-check.
  • Not a model library. You bring the models, from Printables, MakerWorld, Thingiverse or your own folder.
  • Not cloud CAD. No accounts, no storage on someone else’s server, no telemetry. Projects are files on your machine.
  • No team. Solidon3D is built by one person. That means short paths and an answer from the person who built it. It also means no hotline and a roadmap that follows one person.
  • No dimensional guarantees from generated meshes. What comes from text or images is made printable; accurate design happens on ways 1 and 2.
  • The AI brings its own cost. The chat runs on your own API key, typically cents per proposal. Running it locally through Ollama is free, with a graphics card of 8 GB or more; from 10 GB it answers two to three times faster.

Common questions

Who is behind it?

One person: Robert Schneider, RS Digital, Bamberg. Design, program, manual, website and support come from one hand — the reply to your feedback is written by the person who wrote the code.

The fairer question is what happens if that person stops. That is why Solidon3D is built so it does not hold on to you: it runs without an account and without a network, your projects are files on your own machine, and a project file is a ZIP archive with JSON inside — readable without the program. Exports go to formats every slicer and every CAD knows: STL, 3MF, STEP, OBJ.

What happens on 30 October?

From 31 October the demo will no longer start. It says so beforehand: the status bar shows every day how much time is left.

31 October is reserved for the final checks. Solidon3D 1.0 is planned for 1 November 2026 at 10:00 German time; from then on the private and the commercial licence are on sale.

Your files are not affected. They stay where you saved them; nothing is deleted and nothing becomes unreadable. A project file is a ZIP archive with JSON inside and opens without Solidon3D — the next version reads it unchanged.

Why a demo with an end date instead of a trial?

Because that lets it be complete. A version that turns into a viewer after fourteen days shows less on your second project than on your first. This one shows everything until the last day — and then ends, rather than living on as half a program that nobody maintains.

Can I create my own building blocks without programming?

Yes. Build the part once, create parameters for its dimensions and choose Save selection as a part in the catalogue. Creating it computes the block across its whole range; afterwards it sits next to the supplied ones with a preview image and fields.

It stays on your computer — and travels as a recipe inside every project file that uses it: a list of steps and values, not a program. Whoever receives the file can compute with it without installing anything.

Do I need CAD experience?

No. Way 1 gets by with clicking and one sentence in the chat, way 2 with describing and turning numbers. And the drawing itself is built for beginners: every tool says what the next click does, and every constraint explains in one sentence what it holds.

Can I move a hole in a finished STL?

Yes. Holes, pegs, countersinks, domes and sockets are recognised as named features when the file opens; a click shows their measured values as dimensions in the view and in the Selection panel on the right, and changing a number is the operation. Moving, duplicating and removing apply to each of these kinds, turning to all but the spherical face. Fillets and coves that recognisably replace an edge can have their radius changed or be removed; a transition that flows smoothly into its neighbouring faces cannot. Solidon3D shows which actions are available at the selected location.

Do I have to use AI?

No. The chat is one way out of four. Without a key and without Ollama, everything except the chat stays fully usable — menus, the Selection panel, command line and all 142 operations.

Does the AI compute my geometry?

No, and that is the most important sentence on this page. The language model picks operations and parameters — every piece of geometry is computed by code. Each proposal from the agent is exactly one transaction, and a single undo takes it back completely.

Can I drive Solidon3D from Claude Code or another AI tool?

Yes. Solidon3D has a built-in MCP server: a program like Claude Code uses it to call the same operations that sit in the menus. It is off by default, accepts connections only from your own machine (127.0.0.1), takes neither file paths nor source code from outside — and every remote call is a transaction in the history that a single undo takes back completely.

Do my models go anywhere?

Your project files stay on your computer. In chat, the selected language model receives your message, a digest of the scene and the conversation history. If it supports images, rendered views of the model may also be included.

With Anthropic or a remote Ollama server, this data leaves your computer. Ollama running on your own computer processes it locally. Before the first model request, Solidon3D shows the selected destination.

Network connections are also used for device activation, the optional update check and downloads you start. A support message shows its contents before sending. Solidon3D sends no usage statistics and does not store your projects in a cloud.

Which formats does Solidon3D read and write?

It reads STL, 3MF (including assemblies), OBJ and GLB, plus STEP with real faces and edges. It also reads PLY, OFF and glTF — and, as drawings, SVG and DXF, whose outline becomes a solid. It writes STL, 3MF — with colour changes and plate layout —, OBJ, PLY, GLB and STEP; STEP appears in the export dialog as soon as a solid can carry it. GLB is the format for showing: colours and name travel along, and the recipient turns the part in a browser. A project is a ZIP file with project.json inside: openable without Solidon3D.

Does it run on a Mac?

Yes, as separate builds for Apple Silicon and Intel — a package built on arm64 will not start on an Intel Mac, and that covers every machine made before 2020. From version 0.4.1 the packages are notarised with Apple and open on a double-click. An older version reports that macOS cannot verify the file is free of malware and offers “Move to Trash” as the first button: choose “Done” there, then release the package in System Settings under “Privacy & Security” with “Open Anyway”.

How do I install the Flatpak on Linux?

A Flatpak is not a double-click installer but a package your package manager takes in. Open a terminal in the folder you downloaded to and enter: flatpak install ./Solidon3D-*.flatpak

Solidon3D then appears in your application menu. You can also start it with flatpak run de.rsdigital.solidon3d and remove it with flatpak uninstall de.rsdigital.solidon3d. If your system does not know the flatpak command, your distribution installs it under exactly that name.

Will it work with my printer?

Twenty profiles are included: eighteen filament printers, among them Elegoo, Bambu Lab, Prusa, Creality, Anycubic and Sovol, and two resin printers by build volume. You can add your own — what is needed is the build volume and the layer height, plus the nozzle for a filament printer or pixel size and minimum wall for a resin printer. For the fits, one calibration print comes on top.
