Sound familiar?

The four sentences heard most often around a printer — and what Solidon3D does about them.

  • The hole is 0.2 mm too small, and I don't have CAD.

    Click the face, change the number. In a downloaded STL as well — holes, pockets and wall thicknesses are named features, not a cloud of triangles.

  • The print failed at 80 %, because of an island.

    Layer analysis shows it beforehand. Overhangs, islands, walls under two extrusion widths and the support volume — before the slicer spends the time.

  • The lid jams. It fitted on the third try.

    Fits from the material profile. Clearance, press fit or flush are derived from a calibration ladder you print once, not guessed.

  • The part is 40 mm too big for the bed.

    Split and pin in one step. Auto split creates the alignment pins, the holes and the matching fits along with them.

Three ways to a part

All three end in the same scene, the same history and the same inspection report. You decide per part, not per program.

Way 1 — Edit

Change a downloaded model

Drop the file — or drag the link from MakerWorld, Printables or Thingiverse straight out of the browser into the window, skipping the download folder. Read the inspection report, click a face or a hole — then say in the chat what it should become, or pick from the context menu. Compare before/after, apply, export.

Way 2 — Design

Build new, with dimensions

Describe what you need. The agent creates parameters and places proven building blocks — threads, heat-set inserts, hinges. Turn the numbers, the model follows instantly.

Way 3 — Generate

From text or image

A generated mesh runs through the repair chain automatically, gets an inspection report and is split and pinned when needed — until it sits printable on the plate. Needs a locally running ComfyUI with a generator model and a capable graphics card; without both, ways 1 and 2 remain fully usable.

Generated with Meshy, Tripo or Rodin? Download the GLB and drag it in here. What comes out of those is a surface — printable is what it becomes here: repair, wall thicknesses, holes with a fit, a report before you slice.

What makes the difference

Everything here is a screenshot from the running application, or shows exactly what it does.

AI agent

One sentence in the chat, one proposal, one undo

The agent drives the same operations as the menus — there is no shortcut past the geometry. Every proposal shows what it will do before it does it, and is applied as a whole: exactly one transaction, taken back completely by a single undo.

Geometry is computed by code, never by the language model. Every dimension in a proposal is exact — nothing is “roughly generated”.

And that is measured, not promised: a suite of 39 reference requests runs against every change to the agent. As of 2026-08-08, with a local 14-billion-parameter model and no cloud at all: 28 of 39 answered well, 98 % of tool calls valid on the first attempt — and every ambiguous request ended in a question back, not in a guess.

Sketches with constraints

Draw it, dimension it — and every measure may be a parameter

Line, circle, arc, spline, plus finished outlines like slot, bolt circle or hole grid. Ten constraints hold the drawing together — horizontal, perpendicular, tangent, symmetric, distance —, and the bottom line always says what is still free. What becomes of the outline is up to you afterwards: extrude it, cut it in as a pocket, revolve it, sweep it along a bend or span it between two outlines.

The outline enters the B-rep kernel as an exact curve. A drawn circle becomes a round cylinder, not a polygon with many corners.

Inspection report

You learn what will go wrong before you print

Every file you open gets a report: open shells, inverted triangles, walls under two extrusion widths, missing units. Sorted into errors, warnings and hints — and every entry jumps to the place in the model it is talking about.

Every finding carries something you can do about it. An error never ends in “failed” here.

Parametric

Change a number — not rebuild the model

The history is non-destructive: every step stays editable, every number can be turned later. Wall thickness from 2.40 to 3.60? Hollowing, lid, holes and fits recompute — in the same order, with the same result.

Named project parameters instead of scattered numbers: one change in one place, not seven.

Building blocks

A nut pocket in four clicks instead of half an hour

Sixteen tested building blocks — counterbored screw hole, nut pocket, heat-set insert, thread, alignment pin, living hinge, snap fit, magnet pocket. Their dimensions come from a standard-parts table with 40 entries, not from a feeling.

Every block is tested across its whole parameter range: watertight, minimum wall thickness, no self-intersection.

Surfaces

Eight patterns, every one checked for printability

Rib, wave, straight and diamond knurl, honeycomb, dimples, Voronoi and noise — as real geometry and as an exact lattice, not a sampled height field. Otherwise a knurl prints rounded mush instead of sharp diamonds.

Before anything is computed comes the question whether the pattern can exist on this machine at all: ridges narrower than the nozzle and embossings shallower than one layer are refused, not computed.

Layer analysis

See what the printer is going to see

Solidon3D slices the model into layers and measures what follows from that: overhang angles, floating islands, walls under two extrusion widths, bridge spans and support volume. The orientation search proposes the position that produces the least of it.

No slicer of its own — and no mixed numbers: what came from the analysis and what came from G-code is always stated.

Auto split

Too big for the bed is no longer a reason to skip it

Solidon3D finds the cutting plane, splits the part and places alignment pins with their holes. The clearance between them comes from the material profile — no filing, no second attempt. The fit stays in the project as a relationship and is checked again on every later change.

The build volume comes from the printer profile. Sixteen are included, and you can add your own.

Every number a formula

Dialogs that do the arithmetic

Behind every number field there is an fx: instead of 4.20 it may say screw_m4 + clearance. Tolerances take their value from the material profile rather than disappearing into the model as a constant. The three numbers you need are up front — the rest lives under “More settings”.

83 operations, all built the same way: menu, context menu, command line and chat read the same declaration.

What else is inside

  • Exact edges. B-rep kernel for chamfers, fillets and round-trip STEP.
  • Two colours without a second program. Labels raised, recessed, or as a colour change in a single 3MF file.
  • Hand-over to your slicer. Directly, with the matching settings — and its G-code comes back as a cross-check.
  • Calibrate instead of guess. Tolerance ladder, wall thickness ladder and overhang fan on one plate; the measured values go into the material profile.
  • A repair chain for foreign files. Close holes, fix normals, remove degenerate triangles and stray fragments — before anything is computed.
  • Measure, section, explode. Tools in the view that never touch the geometry.
  • AI local or with your own key. Ollama on your own machine or an API key — and without either, everything except the chat keeps working.
  • Remote-controllable via MCP — including from Claude Code. A second program calls the same operations as the menus: off by default, your own machine only, and every remote call is one transaction that a single undo takes back.

When the model comes from an AI

Meshy, Rodin, Tripo, Hunyuan — they turn a sentence into a shape. What they do not turn it into is a part that fits. That is exactly where Solidon3D starts.

  1. Drop in a GLB or STL. Textured as well — the colours survive the import.
  2. Repair chain and report. Holes, flipped normals, degenerate triangles; afterwards it says what will go wrong on the plate.
  3. Map the colours onto your filaments. Onto the number actually loaded in the machine, not onto a maximum.
  4. Split, pin, arrange. Dowels with clearance from the material profile, so the halves find each other again.
  5. 3MF with material groups, straight to the slicer.

What Solidon3D is not

Better said up front:
  • Not a slicer. Solidon3D analyses and prepares; the print file still comes from your slicer — Solidon3D hands over directly and reads its G-code back as a cross-check.
  • Not cloud CAD. No accounts, no storage on someone else's server, no telemetry. Projects are files on your machine.
  • No dimensional guarantees from generated meshes. What comes from text or images is made printable — accurate design happens on ways 1 and 2.
  • The AI brings its own cost. The chat runs on your own API key — the provider bills the model use, typically cents per proposal. Running it locally through Ollama is free, but a capable model (around 14 billion parameters and up) needs a graphics card with 16 GB of memory.

Common questions

What happens on 30 October?

From 31 October the demo will no longer start. It says so beforehand: the status bar shows every day how much time is left.

Your files are not affected. They stay where you saved them; nothing is deleted and nothing becomes unreadable. A project file is a ZIP archive with JSON inside and opens without Solidon3D — the next version reads it unchanged.

Why a demo with an end date instead of a trial?

Because that lets it be complete. A version that turns into a viewer after fourteen days shows less on your second project than on your first. This one shows everything until the last day — and then ends, rather than living on as half a program that nobody maintains.

Do I need CAD experience?

No. Way 1 gets by with clicking and one sentence in the chat, way 2 with describing and turning numbers. Anyone who wants to draw constrained sketches can — but nobody has to.

Do I have to use AI?

No. The chat is one way out of three. Without a key and without Ollama, everything except the chat stays fully usable — menus, context menus, command line and all 83 operations.

Does the AI compute my geometry?

No, and that is the most important sentence on this page. The language model picks operations and parameters — every piece of geometry is computed by code. Each proposal from the agent is exactly one transaction, and a single undo takes it back completely.

Can I drive Solidon3D from Claude Code or another AI tool?

Yes. Solidon3D has a built-in MCP server: a program like Claude Code uses it to call the same operations that sit in the menus. It is off by default, accepts connections only from your own machine (127.0.0.1), takes neither file paths nor source code from outside — and every remote call is a transaction in the history that a single undo takes back completely.

Do my models go anywhere?

Only if you use the chat with a third-party API key — then a digest of the scene goes to that provider: objects with their dimensions, the names of the features, the parameters and the history in brief. No geometry, no files. With Ollama even that stays local. Without the chat, nothing leaves the machine: no accounts, no storage on someone else's server, no telemetry.

Which formats does Solidon3D read and write?

It reads STL, 3MF (including assemblies), OBJ and GLB, plus STEP through the B-rep kernel. It writes STL, 3MF — with colour changes and plate layout —, OBJ, PLY, GLB and STEP; STEP appears in the export dialog as soon as a solid can carry it. GLB is the format for showing: colours and name travel along, and the recipient turns the part in a browser. A project is a ZIP file with project.json inside: openable without Solidon3D.

Does it run on a Mac?

Not for now. Solidon3D comes for Windows and Linux; a macOS build is not a technical question but a decision about signing and maintaining a third platform. If you need one, write to us — that counts.

Will it work with my printer?

Sixteen profiles are included, among them Elegoo, Bambu Lab, Prusa, Creality, Anycubic and Sovol. You can add your own — what is needed is the build volume, the nozzle and the layer height. For the fits, one calibration print comes on top.

What happens after version 1.x?

Every 1.x update is included in the purchase. A later major version would be a new purchase — the version you bought keeps running, it does not stop working, and your projects stay readable.
