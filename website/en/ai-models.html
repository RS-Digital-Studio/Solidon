GLB and STL — printable without CAD experience

When the model comes from an AI

The generators turn a sentence into a shape. What they do not turn it into is a part that fits. That is where Solidon3D starts.

Download for free now

From 1.0 a one-time purchase — no subscription.

  1. Drop in a GLB or STL. Textured as well — the colours survive the import.
  2. Repair chain and report. Holes, flipped normals, degenerate triangles; afterwards it says what will go wrong on the plate.
  3. Map the colours onto your filaments. Onto the number actually loaded in the machine, not onto a maximum.
  4. Split, pin, arrange. Dowels with clearance from the material profile, so the halves find each other again.
  5. 3MF with material groups, straight to the slicer.

Repairing alone is available free in a browser these days, and that is fine — a closed mesh is still not a part that fits. The difference sits behind the repair: dimension, fit, layer analysis, and a history in which every number can still be changed later. And what was once a hole in the mesh, Solidon3D recognises as one again — with a measured diameter, movable and resizable, without a CAD file ever having existed for it.

Your generator remains your choice

A GLB or STL file from any generator you choose goes through the same repair, sizing, printability check, splitting and pinning in Solidon3D as any other model. No CAD experience is needed.

The model behind it is TripoSG. Its source code and model card state the MIT licence; the complete licence and provenance chain of the weights and the models it embeds is currently being verified. Solidon3D sets this route up only at your request and downloads nothing unasked; external GLB and STL files remain fully usable regardless.

Local chat: GPU and CPU measured separately

On 31 August 2026, qwen3:14b completed five out of five full probe instructions correctly with 106 tools. On an NVIDIA RTX 4080 with 16 GB of graphics memory they took 11.8 to 25.2 seconds, with a median of 17 seconds. A 19,641-token prompt was read in 13.1 seconds cold and a warm median of 2.3 seconds; the model was entirely in graphics memory. 7.8 tokens per second and about 42 minutes apply only to the CPU fallback. The application shows whether Ollama is actually using the GPU.
