When the model comes from an AI

Meshy, Rodin, Tripo, Hunyuan — they turn a sentence into a shape. What they do not turn it into is a part that fits. That is where Solidon3D starts.

  1. Drop in a GLB or STL. Textured as well — the colours survive the import.
  2. Repair chain and report. Holes, flipped normals, degenerate triangles; afterwards it says what will go wrong on the plate.
  3. Map the colours onto your filaments. Onto the number actually loaded in the machine, not onto a maximum.
  4. Split, pin, arrange. Dowels with clearance from the material profile, so the halves find each other again.
  5. 3MF with material groups, straight to the slicer.

Repairing alone is available free in a browser these days, and that is fine — a closed mesh is still not a part that fits. The difference sits behind the repair: dimension, fit, layer analysis, and a history in which every number can still be changed later.
