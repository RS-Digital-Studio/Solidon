When the model comes from an AI

The generators turn a sentence into a shape. What they do not turn it into is a part that fits. That is where Solidon3D starts.

  1. Drop in a GLB or STL. Textured as well — the colours survive the import.
  2. Repair chain and report. Holes, flipped normals, degenerate triangles; afterwards it says what will go wrong on the plate.
  3. Map the colours onto your filaments. Onto the number actually loaded in the machine, not onto a maximum.
  4. Split, pin, arrange. Dowels with clearance from the material profile, so the halves find each other again.
  5. 3MF with material groups, straight to the slicer.

Repairing alone is available free in a browser these days, and that is fine — a closed mesh is still not a part that fits. The difference sits behind the repair: dimension, fit, layer analysis, and a history in which every number can still be changed later.

If you have no generator at all

Solidon3D brings the third way with it: a sentence or a picture becomes a body, and it lands in the same history as everything else. That is computed in a local ComfyUI — on your machine, without an account and without an upload.

The model behind it is TripoSG and carries the MIT licence. That is not a detail for lawyers: the most widespread model of its kind, Hunyuan3D, expressly excludes the European Union in its licence. Here the question does not arise.

The times are measured on an NVIDIA card with 8 GB. On Intel and AMD graphics the local paths run slower or entirely on the CPU: the local chat loads its system prompt there at 7.8 tokens per second — 51 minutes before an answer begins. Workarounds exist (IPEX-LLM, ROCm, OpenVINO); we neither ship nor have tested them. What works there is an API key — the application measures this itself and tells you before you wait.

Six sentences, six bodies

Every piece here is a single sentence that Solidon3D handed to the generator — no touching up, no second attempt, no picking one out of ten. They were chosen for what makes such models fail: thin protrusions, pierced walls, undercuts, fine repetition.

And then the work begins

The generator delivered a shape. What turns it into a printable part is three steps in Solidon3D — each in the history, each with a number that can still be changed later.

After that comes what stands at the top of this page: report, overhangs, islands, support, best orientation — and if it does not fit the plate, split and pin it. Generating is the fastest part of the work. The rest is the part that decides the result.
